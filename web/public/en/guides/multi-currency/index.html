Multi-currency totals: reconcile currencies first

Separate cash, holdings and the direction of conversion. Inspect each currency ledger before treating a base-currency total as complete.

From account records to total assets

  1. Cash balance: opening cash adjusted by cash transactions. Transaction amounts describe changes, not an additional stock of assets.
  2. Holding value: quantity and the ledger's reference price determine market value. Cost belongs to its profit/loss calculation and should not be added again to market value.
  3. Account assets: cash plus holdings, aggregated for matching currencies in the server snapshot.
  4. Base-currency total: configured conversion rates combine amounts; currencies without a rate remain explicitly outside that total.

Web views use the same server ledger snapshot. Compare the same timestamp, currency and price source before investigating a discrepancy, rather than making a second client-side calculation.

When allocation figures are usable

The three buckets organise recorded assets against your own target proportions, not a prescribed allocation. First check currency coverage, price validity and record classification. If allocation metrics are unavailable, do not replace missing values with zero or interpret partial-ledger percentages as your complete portfolio.

Common reconciliation differences

  • A missing portion of the total: inspect currencies without configured rates.
  • Cash differs from the brokerage view: check opening cash, transaction directions and record scope.
  • Value changes without a quantity change: examine price source and update time rather than assuming a trade.
  • A reversed rate: specify how much base currency one unit of the original currency buys, not an unlabelled number.
